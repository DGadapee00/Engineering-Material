Structural Mechanics

\[ \sigma = E\varepsilon \qquad \varepsilon = \frac{\Delta L}{L} \qquad \nu = -\frac{\varepsilon_{lat}}{\varepsilon_{long}} \]

True vs. engineering

\[ \sigma_{true}=\sigma_{eng}\,(1+\varepsilon_{eng}) \qquad \varepsilon_{true}=\ln(1+\varepsilon_{eng}) \]

Thermal expansion

\[ \varepsilon=\alpha\,\Delta T \qquad \Delta L = L\,\alpha\,\Delta T \]

Toughness

\[ \text{Toughness}=\int \sigma(\varepsilon)\,d\varepsilon \]

Von Mises

\[ \sigma_{VM}=\sqrt{\frac{(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2+(\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2+(\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2}{2}} \]

\(\sigma_i\) = principal stresses.

Beam in Tension

Normal stress

\[ \sigma=\frac{F}{A} \qquad \Delta L=\frac{FL}{EA} \]
\[ \Delta s=-\frac{FL\nu}{EA} \qquad \text{Energy}=\frac{F^2L}{2EA} \]

Beam in torsion

\[ \tau=\frac{Tc}{J} \qquad \theta=\frac{TL}{GJ} \qquad G=\frac{E}{2(1+\nu)} \]

\(J\) = polar moment of inertia.

Pin in Shear

Single shear shown. For dual shear, divide the shear force by 2.

\[ \tau_{avg}=\frac{V}{A_{pin}} \]
solid cylinder

hollow cylinder

Bolt Calcs

Preload

\[ F=\frac{T}{K\,D} \]

\(T\) torque, \(D\) shank diameter, \(K\) thread factor: \(K\in[0.1,\,0.15]\) for lubed threads, \(K\in[0.15,\,0.2]\) for non-lubed threads.

Thread pullout

\[ F_{shear,ult}=0.4\,\pi\,D\,L_{eng}\,\tau_{ult} \]

\(\tau_{ult}\) from MMPDS. If not available, use \(\sigma_{ult}/\sqrt{3}\). About 40% of threads are generally engaged. \(L_{eng}\) is thread engagement length.

Bolt stress

\[ \sigma=\frac{F_{preload}+F_{external}\,n\,\dfrac{k_b}{k_b+k_c}}{A_{tensile}} \]

Valid while \(F_{external}\lt F_{preload}\).

\[ A_{tensile}=\frac{F_{bolt,ult,tensile}}{\sigma_{ult,tensile}} \]

\(n\) = load introduction factor ≈ 0.5. \(k_b/(k_b+k_c)\approx 0.2\text{–}0.5\). \(k_b\) fastener stiffness, \(k_c\) clamp stiffness.

Weld Stress

Plates joined with parallel fillet welds. \(h\) = weld height, \(w\) = weld width. Minimum shear thickness for a parallel double fillet is \(h/\sqrt{2}\).

\[ \tau\approx\frac{F/2}{w\,h/\sqrt{2}} \]

Compare \(\tau\) against \(\tau_{ult}\). Include weld knockdown (steel knockdown ≈ 0.8). Assumes \(t_{weld}\ll L_{geom}\).

Pressurized Spherical Shell

\[ \sigma_{hoop}=\sigma_1=\sigma_2=\frac{PR}{2t} \qquad R\gg t \]
\[ \sigma_{radial}=-P \qquad \left(\tfrac{\sigma_{rad}}{\sigma_{hoop}}\approx 0\right) \]
\[ \Delta R=\frac{PR^2(1-\nu)}{2Et}\ \text{(radial growth)} \]
\[ \varepsilon_{hoop}=\frac{PR(1-\nu)}{2Et} \]
\[ \Delta R=R\,\alpha\,\Delta T\ \text{(thermal growth)} \]
\[ \text{Energy}=\frac{\pi P^2R^4(1-\nu)}{Et} \]

Pressurized Pipes w/ End Caps

\[ \sigma_{radial}=\sigma_3=-P \qquad \left(\tfrac{\sigma_3}{\sigma_1}\approx 0\right) \]

Thick-walled formulation

\[ \sigma_{hoop}=\sigma_1=P\,\frac{R_o^2+R_i^2}{R_o^2-R_i^2} \]

Thin wall

\[ \sigma_{hoop}\approx\frac{PR_i}{t} \qquad \sigma_{axial}=\sigma_2\approx\frac{PR_i}{2t} \]

Valid for \(R_{mean}/t>10\).

\[ \Delta L=\frac{PR_iL}{Et}\left(\frac12-\nu\right) \]
\[ \Delta R=\frac{PR^2}{Et}\left(1-\frac{\nu}{2}\right) \]

The axial contraction term comes from the Poisson effect.

Pressurized Circular Plate

Fixed perimeter, for \(R/t\ge 30\).

\[ \delta=\frac{3}{16}\,\frac{PR^4(1-\nu^2)}{Et^3} \]
\[ \sigma=\frac{3}{4}\,P\left(\frac{R}{t}\right)^2 \]

Rotating shaft radial growth

\[ \Delta R_o=\frac{\rho\omega^2R_o}{4E}\Big[(1-\nu)R_o^2+(3+\nu)R_i^2\Big] \]

Thermal stress, thin-walled cylinder (unconstrained)

\[ \sigma_{th}=\frac{\alpha E\,\Delta T}{2(1-\nu)} \]

Bearing Stress

For a single clevis. Divide by 2 for a dual clevis.

\[ \sigma_{bearing}=\frac{F}{D\,t} \]

\(\sigma_{bru}\) is a function of \(e/D\). \(e\) = distance from the hole center to the edge.

Shear tear-out

\[ \tau=\frac{F}{2\,e\,t} \]

\(2et\) is the total area of the shear plane.

Fluid Mechanics

\(U\) = velocity.

Continuity

\[ \rho A U=\text{constant}=\dot m \]

Momentum (Bernoulli's equation, inviscid, steady)

\[ \frac{P}{\rho}+\frac{U^2}{2}+a\,z=\text{constant} \]

\(z\) = height, \(a\) = acceleration.

Energy (adiabatic, steady, no work)

\[ C_p(T_e-T_i)+\frac{U_e^2-U_i^2}{2}+a\,(z_e-z_i)=0 \]

Control volume, pipe flow

\[ \Sigma F=(\dot m_e V_e-\dot m_i V_i)+(P_eA_e-P_iA_i) \]
\[ D_{hydraulic}=\frac{4A}{P} \qquad \tau(r)=\mu\frac{du}{dr} \]
\[ Re=\frac{UD}{\nu}=\frac{\rho U D}{\mu}=\frac{4\dot m}{\pi D\mu} \]
\[ F_L=\tfrac12 C_L\,\rho A U^2 \qquad F_D=\tfrac12 C_D\,\rho A U^2 \]

Incompressible flow

\[ \dot m=C_D\,A\sqrt{2\rho\,\Delta P_{tot}} \]

\(\Delta P_{tot}=P_{1,tot}-P_{2,tot}\).

Pressure Loss in a Pipe w/ Laminar Flow

\[ \Delta P=f\,\frac{L}{D}\,\frac{\rho U^2}{2} \qquad f=\frac{64}{Re_D} \]

\(f\) = friction loss factor. \(L/D\) = length over diameter ratio.

Cavitation check

\[ K=\frac{2\,\Delta P}{\rho U^2} \]

Cavitation is initiated for \(K\in[0.2,\,1.5]\).

Isentropic relations

\[ \frac{P_2}{P_1}=\left(\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}\right)^{\gamma}=\left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}} \]

Dynamics

\(\zeta\) = damping ratio. \(\omega_n\) = natural frequency [rad/s].

Governing equation of motion (1-DOF oscillator)

\[ F(t)=m\ddot x+b\dot x+kx \]
\[ \frac{F(t)}{m}=\ddot x+2\zeta\omega_n\dot x+\omega_n^2x \]
\[ \omega\,[\text{rad/s}]=2\pi f\,[\text{Hz}] \]
\[ \omega_n=\omega_{undamped}=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} \]

\(\omega_n\) depends on the stiffness-to-mass ratio.

\[ \omega_d=\omega_n\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}\ \text{(damped frequency)} \]
\[ \omega_d\approx\omega_n\ \text{generally} \]
\[ Q=\frac{A_{max}}{A_0}=\frac{1}{2\zeta} \]

Doubling the damping ratio halves the amplitude.

Damping sourceζ
All metals≈ 1%
Silicone damping≈ 2–3%
Fluid damping≈ 5–10%

Time-domain response to a step input

\[ t_s\approx\frac{4.6}{\zeta\omega_n}\ \ (1\%\ \text{settling time}) \]
\[ A_{overshoot}=e^{-\pi\zeta/\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}} \]

Estimate the damping ratio by fitting \(A\,e^{-\zeta\omega_n t}\) to test data.

Analysis Checklist

  • Define analysis goals and start with a free body diagram.
  • Estimate results with hand calculations and double check units at all steps.
  • Obtain material properties and factors of safety from an approved source.
  • Tabulate all loads, temperatures, accelerations, and number of cycles.
  • Evaluate design at worst case load and thermal conditions (incl. transients).
  • Sanity check material cards and ensure they cover the full range of temps.
  • Include relevant thermal, weld, and casting knockdown factors.
  • Scale loads for symmetry/periodicity.
  • Account for fluid/gas mass in lines.
  • Ensure relevant blow-off loads are included (see free body diagram).
  • Ensure blends/fillets are large enough to avoid fatigue and the mesh adequately captures stress concentrations (3 elements across radii).
  • Perform mesh convergence study.
  • Exaggerate deformations to verify boundary conditions and constraints.
  • Ensure parts in sliding are resistant to galling.
  • Use principal stress for brittle materials (elongation < 3%).
  • Account for worst case tolerances and fit-up loads.
  • Assess creep for sustained loads at high temperatures.
  • Ensure fatigue calcs are corrected for temperature and include vibration.
  • Verify human-rated factors of safety are used where personnel are present.
  • Assess margins of safety on yield, ult., elong., stability, fatigue, and gapping.
  • Check results with hand calcs (ΣF=0, therm. strain, hoop stress, mass).
  • Anchor model (strain gages, test data) and obtain peer review.
  • Document inputs, assumptions, and results in a rev. controlled archive.
